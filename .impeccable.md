# Design Context — SharkDeck

## Users
Keyboard hobbyists owning an Attack Shark K86 who want the 128×128 TFT and the knob to do
things the stock software never offered. They tinker: upload art, rebind the knob, script macros.
Context of use: sitting at a desk, often mid-build, switching between dark room and daylight.

## Brand Personality
Precise, tool-like, quietly opinionated. "Tactile workshop instrument" — not a toy, not a toy store.
Three words: **precise, engineered, unfussy**.

## Aesthetic Direction
GNOME / Yaru (Ubuntu) flat light theme as the primary, Adwaita-dark as the paired dark theme.
Flat surfaces, hairline borders, one saturated accent (Yaru orange `#e95420`) used sparingly.
No glassmorphism, no blur, no gradient text, no neon-on-dark, no decorative sparkline charts.
Type: system-humanist sans (Ubuntu/Cantarell/Inter stack) for UI; tabular/mono for numbers and paths.
The *screen content itself* (templates, watch faces) is where personality lives: pixel-art,
limited palette, deliberate composition.

## Design Principles
1. **Chrome recedes, content sings.** The app frame is neutral and flat; the keyboard screen is the
   expressive surface. Never let UI gradients compete with panel artwork.
2. **Honest state.** The hardware cannot read back its own display — say so, and mirror what we push
   instead of faking a read-back. Empty states teach, error copy is plain.
3. **One accent, earned.** Yaru orange marks the single primary action per view; everything else is
   neutral. No side-stripe accents, no glow rings, no "AI tell" left-edge bars.
4. **Data where it belongs.** Numbers (slots, Hz, sizes, bytes) in tabular mono; labels in sentence case;
   never uppercase walls of text.
5. **Pixel discipline.** Templates snap to integer pixels at the panel's real resolution; previews are
   shown 1:1 and nearest-neighbour upscaled, never blurry.
